ml-clusters: mass-scaled masks, ClusterNet, and bug audit

Branch refactor-centernet · updated 2026-11-20 · WebSky simulations with ACT DR6 beams and noise, f090/f150/f220

Summary. The cluster masks now scale with θ500c and the training targets ignore halos of 1–2×1014 M☉. The Faster R-CNN has been replaced by ClusterNet, a 2.6M-parameter ConvNeXt U-Net with CenterNet heads, and evaluation uses a leak-free spatial split with sky-level one-to-one matching. On clean WebSky maps with the corrected f150 CIB, ClusterNet reaches test F1 0.874 (P 0.883, C 0.865), or 0.886 with the mass head (§3). Two data bugs were found and fixed: f150/f220 sat 1 px off f090, and the f150 map used the 90 GHz CIB. New (§4): with the ACT DR6 beam and DR6-like noise added, a model trained on clean maps collapses (F1 0.05). Training on beam + noise maps gives F1 0.49, finds ≥98% of clusters above 6×1014 M☉, and still scores 0.79 on clean maps. Train on observed-like maps. New (§4.2): on 1,535 deg² of blind 4° sky, ClusterNet (n2) ties the nemo matched filter run with the ACT DR5 setup on the same maps: both are 90% complete at true M500c = 3.8×1014 M☉ (±0.1) at 97% purity, which reproduces the published ACT DR5 limit. Below 3.5×1014 ClusterNet is more complete (0.47 vs 0.34 at 2.9×1014). Getting there exposed a bug in our noise maps: noise up to 1019 μK at the DR6 footprint edges (§5.4, fixed).

1. Mass-scaled cluster masks

The mask (training target) radius for each cluster is now its angular size θ500c = R500c / dA(z). WebSky provides M200m; M500c and R500c follow from an NFW profile with the Duffy et al. (2008) concentration c200m(M, z), solved by vectorised bisection (agrees with colossus to 1e-4) in the WebSky cosmology (flat ΛCDM, Ωm = 0.31, h = 0.68). Radii are clipped to [1′, 6′]: the floor keeps every mask resolvable (≥ 2 px at 0.5′), the ceiling stops the rare very-low-z halos from covering the tile. Median θ500c for M200m > 2×1014 is 1.65′ (the old fixed radius was 1.2′) with a long tail to > 6′.

Halos with 1014 ≤ M200m < 2×1014 are real SZ sources but below the positive cut, so their discs (and those of positives centred just outside the tile) are ignore regions: the loss neither rewards nor penalises detections there.

The notebook implementation this replaces (halo-masks.ipynb) had several unit errors that together made masks about 0.3/(1+z) of θ200c, i.e. ~5× too small at z = 0.5 (details in the bug table, items M1–M2).

2. New network: ClusterNet

  • Architecture: ConvNeXt U-Net (stages 32/64/128/256 channels, depths 2/2/6/2, 2.6 M parameters) with a full-resolution stem, plus two CenterNet-style heads: a cluster-centre heatmap (penalty-reduced focal loss with ignore weights) and log radius (L1 at centres). Detections are 3×3 local maxima with sub-pixel centroiding; each comes with a predicted radius.
  • Why: clusters are small (2–12 px), round, and mostly isolated. An anchor-free, full-resolution, fully convolutional detector fits that better than Faster R-CNN with ImageNet normalisation and 120→800 px upsampling. It works on any tile size and any number of frequency channels, so it is ready for larger maps and extra bands.
  • Training: AdamW (lr 2e-3, wd 0.05), linear warm-up + cosine decay, bf16 autocast, torch.compile, batch 64, random dihedral (8-fold) augmentation; ~800 img/s on the A6000 (~4 min per epoch over 145k tiles).
  • Inputs: per-tile median removed, divided by a global per-channel robust σ (keeps the relative amplitudes between frequencies that carry the tSZ spectral signature), then compressed with 3·asinh(x/3) to tame point sources.
  • Data: the 186,784 FITS tiles are packed into one 32 GB memmap; targets are rendered on the fly from the halo catalogue, so mask settings can change without regenerating any files.

3. Evaluation

Protocol. Detections within 2 px of a tile edge are dropped; the rest are converted to the sky with each tile's WCS, de-duplicated across overlapping tiles (sky NMS at 2′) and matched one-to-one (greedy by score) to catalogue halos with M200m > 2×1014 within 2′. Completeness uses halos inside the searched footprint. The score threshold is chosen on the validation split (max F1) and then applied to test. "Purity (incl.)" also counts detections of 1014–2×1014 halos as real.

3.1 Leak-free spatial split

Train/val/test are HEALPix nside=4 regions (~215 deg²) in an 80/10/10 ratio; tiles that straddle a region boundary are discarded, so no sky pixel appears in two splits (145,196 / 15,325 / 14,775 tiles).

3.2 Head-to-head with the legacy Faster R-CNN

The legacy model was trained on the old split, whose test tiles share sky with its training tiles, so no clean test set exists for it. For a like-for-like comparison, ClusterNet was also trained on exactly the legacy training tiles (original, misaligned channels) and both models were scored with the same code on the legacy test tiles (18,723 tiles centred on the lowest-mass 10% of halos). Both numbers here are optimistic because of the shared sky.

Did the old metric code mislead? Hardly, on this data. Running the pre-refactor purity/completeness code verbatim on the same legacy detections (5,000 legacy test tiles, score ≥ 0.5) gives P = 0.908, C = 0.961, against P = 0.906, C = 0.961 from the new scoring. The duplicate-counting and cos δ bugs are real, but most of the clusters here are well separated, so they hardly change the numbers. The large effect in the old evaluation was the sky-overlapping train/test split (E1), not the matching code.

3.3 Ablation: v1 → v4

v1 is trained on the original tiles, whose f150/f220 channels are 1 px off (§5.1). v2 is the same model on realigned tiles, and the gain (F1 0.921 → 0.931) is the alignment fix. v3 uses tiles re-cut from the corrected f150 map, which has ~5.7× more CIB at 150 GHz (§5.2–5.3). F1 drops to 0.874 because the corrected map is harder: the earlier numbers were optimistic. v3 is the honest clean-map baseline. v4 adds a log-mass head trained on every halo above 1014, which slightly helps detection (F1 0.886) and recovers M200m with a robust scatter of 0.03 dex (bias −0.01 dex) on these noise-free maps:

4. Beam and noise: train on clean or on observed maps?

Observed maps. Each clean WebSky band is convolved with the ACT DR6 coadd beam (beam_f*_tform.txt, spherical harmonics up to ℓ = 21600; f090/f150/f220 FWHM ≈ 2.5/1.6/1.1′) and given one realisation of DR6-like noise: white noise from the DR6 daynight temperature ivar, plus an atmospheric component scaled to the local depth. The spectrum of the atmospheric component is measured, relative to white, from DR6 daynight − night source-free difference maps in 7–8 patches of 10°. The noise is zero outside the DR6 footprint (48.8% of the sky), and only tiles fully inside it are used here: 73,561 train, 6,044 val and 8,160 test tiles from the same HEALPix split. Median tile depth is 13.7/13.5/68 μK-arcmin. Code: mlclusters/instrument.py, scripts/fit_act_noise.py, scripts/make_observed_maps.py, scripts/footprint_split.py; the whole pipeline is scripts/run_noise_experiment.sh.

{{table:noise}}
  • Clean-trained models do not transfer. v3 drops to F1 0.57 with the beam alone and to 0.05 with noise. On noisy maps it misses most clusters, even above 1015 M☉. The beam-only model is just as brittle in reverse (0.31 on clean maps). On noisy maps v3 and n1 fire on noise peaks everywhere: their purity stays at 2–4% at every threshold (P–C curve below), so no choice of threshold can rescue them.
  • Training on beam + noise is best on noisy data and the most robust overall. n2 nearly ties the beam-only model on beam maps (0.848 vs 0.852) and still scores 0.79 on clean maps.
  • The noisy F1 of ~0.49 is a detectability limit, not a training problem. Train and val loss are equal (1.98), so there is no overfitting, and n2's validation F1 levels off at ~0.48 after four epochs (training curves in §3). n2 finds 98–99% of clusters above 6×1014 and 83% at 4–6×1014, but only 27% at 2–3×1014, which make up most of the target catalogue and are often below the noise. Scoring against a mass or S/N-limited catalogue would be the fairer test.
  • Pretraining on clean maps barely helps. n3 (v3 fine-tuned) gains a little completeness at low mass but loses purity, and its purity–completeness curve lies right on top of n2's (trained from scratch). The small differences in the table only reflect which threshold was chosen.

4.1 Against the ACT DR5 cluster catalogue

F1 against every halo above M200m = 2×1014 is a harsh target: no method finds clusters down to that mass in ACT-depth data. The state of the art, the ACT DR5 SZ catalogue (Hilton et al. 2021), is 90% complete for M500c > 3.8×1014 M☉ at z = 0.5, for SNR2.4 > 5. At that cut, signal-free simulations give a false-detection fraction of ~3% (75 false over 13,211 deg², i.e. 0.0057 deg−2). scripts/compare_act.py puts our test results on the same footing:

  • Completeness against true M500c (from the WebSky catalogue), for 0.3 < z < 0.7.
  • Purity counting any matched M200m > 1014 halo as real. A real survey's optical follow-up would confirm those.
  • False detections per deg² over the union of the test tiles (1,804 deg²). The 1° test tiles, centred on every massive halo, cover roughly 90% of the test regions, so this is close to a blind search.

Measured this way, the model trained on beam + noise is on par with the ACT DR5 matched-filter catalogue: 90% complete at true M500c ≈ 3.5–3.7×1014 at ACT-like purity, and ≥ 98% complete above 3.8×1014. This is not yet a like-for-like win, for three reasons:

  • ACT's 3.8×1014 is on the UPP (X-ray-calibrated) mass scale. Their weak-lensing calibration factor of 0.71 suggests true masses are higher, which would put ACT's limit near 5×1014 in true mass. That would favour us; the paper's exact convention should be checked.
  • Our maps are easier than real ACT data in several ways. They use DR6 depth (median 13.5 μK-arcmin at f150, versus < 30 μK-arcmin for DR5) and add f220. The noise is idealised: one fixed realisation, isotropic, uncorrelated between bands. There are no masks, and no foreground or source mismatch between the simulation and the sky.
  • The fair benchmark is a matched filter (the nemo pipeline) run on these same simulated maps: done in §4.2.

Caveats. There is one fixed noise realisation per band, so the model may partly memorise it; drawing fresh noise each epoch would fix that. The noise is uncorrelated between bands and isotropic, with no scan-direction stripes. The maps have no map-maker transfer function and no point-source masking.

4.2 Blind search against a matched filter (nemo, ACT DR5 setup)

The 1° tiles are centred on halos, so §4.1 is close to, but not quite, a blind search. Here both methods search the same sky blindly:

  • ClusterNet runs on 4°×4° TAN patches (481×481 px, 0.5° overlap) tiling the test footprint (scripts/make_sky_patches.py). Detections within 20 px of a patch border are dropped and the rest merged across patches at 2′ (scripts/eval_sky.py --model). The network is fully convolutional; on the same cluster its peak score is identical in 1°, 2° and 4° cutouts.
  • nemo (simonsobs/nemo, Hilton's matched-filter pipeline) runs with the ACT DR5 cluster-search setup (scripts/make_nemo_config.py --mode dr5): 98 + 150 GHz only, 16 Arnaud (UPP) filters (M500c = 1–8×1014, z = 0.2–1.2), noise estimated from the map in 10°×5° tiles, S/N from Arnaud_M2e14_z0p4, one pass. As in DR5, point sources above 10 mJy at 150 GHz are masked and filled before filtering. They come from an f150 nemo source search on the same maps (446 sources; holes of 320″ growing to 12′ with flux), and pixels shallower than 10% of the median depth are cut.
  • Same area for both. Scoring uses nemo's search area (test-tile union minus depth cut and source holes) trimmed by 12′ from its edges: 1,535 deg². The union of 1° tiles has a very ragged edge, and nemo makes spurious detections along survey-mask boundaries. Untrimmed, half its false S/N > 5 detections lie within 4′ of an edge, and none has tSZ signal in the true y map. Purity at S/N > 5 rises from 0.91 (no trim) to 0.96 / 0.98 / 0.984 / 0.986 at 4′ / 8′ / 12′ / 16′, so 12′ removes the edge effect. A contiguous survey mask would avoid it in a real survey.

  • nemo reproduces ACT DR5. At S/N ≈ 5 it is 90% complete at 3.8–4.0×1014 with a false rate close to the paper's, which validates the simulation, the setup and the scoring.
  • ClusterNet ties nemo at the 90% point. M90 agrees to within one bootstrap σ at both operating points, and the M90-versus-false-rate curves overlap from 10−3 to 1 false detection per deg² (right panel; nemo's catalogue stops near 0.3 deg−2 at its S/N 4 cut).
  • ClusterNet is more complete below 3.5×1014. At the same purity it finds 47% vs 34% of clusters at 2.9×1014 and 25% vs 15% at 2.4×1014 (left panel). It uses f220 and is not tied to a fixed set of UPP templates; we have not separated these effects.
  • Retraining on the fixed maps changes nothing measurable (n2 old vs retrained: test F1 0.488 vs 0.489 on the v6 test tiles; M90 equal within errors). The ~1% of training tiles with exploded noise (§5.4) did no harm.

First attempt and what went wrong. nemo was first run with its ACT DR6 example setup (98/150/220 GHz, two point-source passes, two cluster passes; 9 h on 16 cores). Its catalogue was unusable: 22,700 objects at S/N > 5 and 13% purity. In 40 of 95 tiles the matched filter was ~1010 too small and yc came out at 103–109. The cause was the noise bug of §5.4: tiles whose 1° overlap reached the exploded pixels produced point sources of up to 1012 Jy, and subtracting those wrecked the filters of the tile and its neighbours. On the 56 unaffected tiles that run gave the same conclusion as above (nemo M90 3.76 vs n2 3.86×1014 at 97% purity).

5. Data problems found

5.1 Frequency channels misaligned by one pixel

In every existing tile, the f150 and f220 channels sit 1 px (0.5′) higher in Dec than f090 and the stored WCS. make_stamp passed proj="tan" only for the first frequency; pixell's default for the others is a CAR thumbnail with a different reference pixel. I confirmed this by re-cutting tiles at the same centres with the new make_tiles: after a +1-row shift of f150/f220, old and new tiles agree to 0.8% / 4.5% rms (versus 12% / 68% without the shift). scripts/realign_channels.py writes a corrected copy.

5.3 Regenerated f150 map

The map was rebuilt with scripts/make_websky_map.py 150 (5 min, 23 GB RAM) and written to /mnt/welch/USERS/jorlo/ml-clusters/maps/websky_f150_map_cibfix.fits; the original in the shared WebSky directory is untouched. In three 4°×4° test patches the difference new − old equals 2594.7 × (cib_153 − cib_90.2) with a 0.000 μK residual, i.e. the CIB term is the only change, and repeating the template regression on the new map now selects cib_153 (factor 2.58e3). The change is ~22 μK rms per pixel, dominated by bright dusty sources. All 186,784 tiles were re-cut from the corrected maps at the original centres with the fixed make_tiles (TAN projection for every channel, so no realignment is needed) into packed_v3_cibfix; models v3 and v4 below are trained on these.

5.4 Noise up to 1019 μK at the DR6 footprint edges

The DR6 ivar maps have edge pixels with ivar down to 5×10−38 μK−2. Our noise is white with variance 1/ivar, so the v1 observed maps (maps/observed/) held noise up to 1019 μK there. Real maps contain nothing like it. It touched 6,735 of 186,784 tiles (698 train, 56 val and 58 test tiles in the ACT split, and 26 of 241 4° patches) and broke every Fourier-space filter whose tile reached it (§4.2). Fix: make_observed_maps.py --ivar_floor 0.01 (instrument.floor_ivar) treats pixels with ivar below 1% of the median as unobserved, which removes 2.2% of the observed area. The maximum noise is now 1.7×103 / 3.3×103 / 1.7×104 μK at f090 / f150 / f220. New maps are in maps/observed_v2/, with tiles in packed_v6_beam_noise (8,076 ACT test tiles instead of 8,160) and patches in sky_patches_4deg_v6; build with scripts/run_v6_data.sh. The §4 noise table was made on the v1 maps; n2 scores the same on v6 (F1 0.488).

7. Caveats and next steps

  • §1–3 numbers are for clean maps and are far above what is achievable on ACT data; §4 is the realistic setting.
  • Draw fresh noise realisations on the fly each epoch, and add noise correlated between bands, scan-direction anisotropy, the map-maker transfer function and point-source masking.
  • Find out where ClusterNet's extra low-mass completeness comes from (f220, template freedom, or something else): run nemo with f220 and ClusterNet without it.
  • Run both methods on a contiguous footprint (e.g. the whole ACT area, scoring held-out regions) so no edge trim is needed.
  • Evaluate against a mass- or S/N-limited catalogue, and report completeness against local depth (per-tile depths are in split_act.npz).
  • Natural extensions: a log M500c regression head, a mass/SNR-dependent positive cut, and inference on large maps rather than per-cluster tiles.
