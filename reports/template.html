1. Mass-scaled cluster masks

The mask (training target) radius for each cluster is now its angular size θ500c = R500c / dA(z). WebSky provides M200m; M500c and R500c follow from an NFW profile with the Duffy et al. (2008) concentration c200m(M, z), solved by vectorised bisection (agrees with colossus to 1e-4) in the WebSky cosmology (flat ΛCDM, Ωm = 0.31, h = 0.68). Radii are clipped to [1′, 6′]: the floor keeps every mask resolvable (≥ 2 px at 0.5′), the ceiling stops the rare very-low-z halos from covering the tile. Median θ500c for M200m > 2×1014 is 1.65′ (the old fixed radius was 1.2′) with a long tail to > 6′.

Halos with 1014 ≤ M200m < 2×1014 are real SZ sources but below the positive cut, so their discs (and those of positives centred just outside the tile) are ignore regions: the loss neither rewards nor penalises detections there.

{{fig:sizes}} {{fig:masks}}

The notebook implementation this replaces (halo-masks.ipynb) had several unit errors that together made masks about 0.3/(1+z) of θ200c, i.e. ~5× too small at z = 0.5 (details in the bug table, items M1–M2).

2. New network: ClusterNet

  • Architecture: ConvNeXt U-Net (stages 32/64/128/256 channels, depths 2/2/6/2, 2.6 M parameters) with a full-resolution stem, plus two CenterNet-style heads: a cluster-centre heatmap (penalty-reduced focal loss with ignore weights) and log radius (L1 at centres). Detections are 3×3 local maxima with sub-pixel centroiding; each comes with a predicted radius.
  • Why: clusters are small (2–12 px), round, and mostly isolated. An anchor-free, full-resolution, fully convolutional detector fits that better than Faster R-CNN with ImageNet normalisation and 120→800 px upsampling. It works on any tile size and any number of frequency channels, so it is ready for larger maps and extra bands.
  • Training: AdamW (lr 2e-3, wd 0.05), linear warm-up + cosine decay, bf16 autocast, torch.compile, batch 64, random dihedral (8-fold) augmentation; ~800 img/s on the A6000 (~4 min per epoch over 145k tiles).
  • Inputs: per-tile median removed, divided by a global per-channel robust σ (keeps the relative amplitudes between frequencies that carry the tSZ spectral signature), then compressed with 3·asinh(x/3) to tame point sources.
  • Data: the 186,784 FITS tiles are packed into one 32 GB memmap; targets are rendered on the fly from the halo catalogue, so mask settings can change without regenerating any files.

3. Evaluation

Protocol. Detections within 2 px of a tile edge are dropped; the rest are converted to the sky with each tile's WCS, de-duplicated across overlapping tiles (sky NMS at 2′) and matched one-to-one (greedy by score) to catalogue halos with M200m > 2×1014 within 2′. Completeness uses halos inside the searched footprint. The score threshold is chosen on the validation split (max F1) and then applied to test. "Purity (incl.)" also counts detections of 1014–2×1014 halos as real.

3.1 Leak-free spatial split

Train/val/test are HEALPix nside=4 regions (~215 deg²) in an 80/10/10 ratio; tiles that straddle a region boundary are discarded, so no sky pixel appears in two splits (145,196 / 15,325 / 14,775 tiles).

3.2 Head-to-head with the legacy Faster R-CNN

The legacy model was trained on the old split, whose test tiles share sky with its training tiles, so no clean test set exists for it. For a like-for-like comparison, ClusterNet was also trained on exactly the legacy training tiles (original, misaligned channels) and both models were scored with the same code on the legacy test tiles (18,723 tiles centred on the lowest-mass 10% of halos). Both numbers here are optimistic because of the shared sky.

{{table:metrics_legacy}}

Did the old metric code mislead? Hardly, on this data. Running the pre-refactor purity/completeness code verbatim on the same legacy detections (5,000 legacy test tiles, score ≥ 0.5) gives P = 0.908, C = 0.961, against P = 0.906, C = 0.961 from the new scoring. The duplicate-counting and cos δ bugs are real, but most of the clusters here are well separated, so they hardly change the numbers. The large effect in the old evaluation was the sky-overlapping train/test split (E1), not the matching code.

4. Data problems found

4.1 Frequency channels misaligned by one pixel

In every existing tile, the f150 and f220 channels sit 1 px (0.5′) higher in Dec than f090 and the stored WCS. make_stamp passed proj="tan" only for the first frequency; pixell's default for the others is a CAR thumbnail with a different reference pixel. I confirmed this by re-cutting tiles at the same centres with the new make_tiles: after a +1-row shift of f150/f220, old and new tiles agree to 0.8% / 4.5% rms (versus 12% / 68% without the shift). scripts/realign_channels.py writes a corrected copy.

6. Caveats and next steps

  • The WebSky maps have no beam and no noise, so absolute purity/completeness here are far higher than achievable on ACT data. Add per-band beams and realistic noise (ideally ACT DR6 inverse-variance maps) before applying to real maps.
  • Regenerate the f150 map with the CIB fix and re-cut the tiles with make_tiles.py (aligned, centred 121×121 tiles).
  • Natural extensions: a log M500c regression head, a mass/SNR-dependent positive cut, and inference on large maps rather than per-cluster tiles.
